Protein & Movement: Building Habits That Last

GLP-1 helps you eat less. What you eat, and how much you move, is what decides whether the result lasts — and whether you lose fat or muscle along the way.

Why protein matters more during treatment

When you eat less overall, your body doesn't automatically protect muscle over fat. Without enough protein, a meaningful share of what you lose can be muscle, not fat — which means a slower metabolism and a harder time keeping the weight off later. It's why protein intake is tracked as part of your program, not left to chance.

Why staying mobile matters

Movement does two things medication alone can't: it helps preserve the muscle and strength protein protects, and it supports the metabolic changes — better insulin sensitivity, cardiovascular health — that make results durable, not just visible on a scale.

What protein looks like, day to day

Many doctors use a general guide of roughly 1.2–1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day during active weight loss — your doctor will set the target that's right for you. The practical habit is simpler than the number sounds:

  • Include a protein source at every meal — dal, paneer, curd/yogurt, eggs, chicken, fish, sprouts, or a supplement if your diet needs it.
  • Spread it across the day rather than one large portion at dinner — your body uses it better that way.
  • Since GLP-1 reduces appetite, what you do eat matters more than usual — less room for empty calories, more reason to make each meal count.

What mobility looks like — no gym membership required

  • A daily walk, even in short stretches, does most of the heavy lifting for most people.
  • Two to three sessions a week of light resistance or strength work — bodyweight exercises count — help protect muscle specifically.
  • Building movement into your existing routine (stairs instead of the lift, walking calls, parking a little further away) adds up more reliably than one intense weekly workout.

How this fits into your program

This isn't separate advice you're left to figure out on your own — it's part of what your doctor and the WhatsApp Vault coaching track alongside your dose and your labs. Progress here counts as much as the number on the scale.
